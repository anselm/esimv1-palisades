This is one run of our own fire engine on the Palisades fire, from the morning of 7 January 2025 to 12 January. Drag the bar under the picture to move through the five days, or press play. Everything on this page follows the moment the bar shows.

The model divides the ground into 30-metre cells. Each cell has a height, a land cover and, where there are any, buildings. A burning cell heats its neighbours, more strongly downwind and uphill, and a neighbour catches when it has taken enough heat. Embers can start new fires ahead of the front.

Fire agencies mapped the edge of the fire about once a day. At each mapping the model's burned area is compared with the mapped one. Satellites give a second, coarser record between mappings.

No variant matches the first night and then stops. The settings that keep pace with the first day overrun the record on the second, because the model has no way for a fire to go out.

State inspectors recorded what happened to each structure after the fire. The model's fire is scored against those inspections: of the structures recorded destroyed, how many did the model's fire reach, and of those it reached, how many were in fact destroyed.

The second share stays near one half whatever the setting, which is the share of inspected structures that were destroyed. Nothing the model holds tells a house that burned from the one beside it that did not.

The wind and the dryness of dead fuel drive the spread. Both are read from weather records and are the same in every variant of the scenario unless the variant says otherwise.

  • Every constant of the fire engine is a guess. One of them, the multiplier on the heat a cell needs before it catches, is the knob the variants turn.
  • Nobody fights the fire unless the variant says so.
  • The wind does not bend around the terrain, and the fire does not change the wind.
  • The fuel table is recalled from memory of Scott and Burgan (2005) and has not been checked against it.
  • The first mapped perimeter is a hand-drawn triangle.
  • The mapped lines carry the time they were entered, which is later than the work on the ground.
